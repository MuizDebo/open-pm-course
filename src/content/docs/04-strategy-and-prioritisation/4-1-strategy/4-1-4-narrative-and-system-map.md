## Introduction

This project produces the two artifacts that every later project in the course builds on:
a written strategy narrative and a system map. The narrative turns everything you learned
in Course 3 about your product's users and market into a set of beliefs and one bet. The
map shows the loops the bet depends on and where you intend to push.

Working PMs produce these documents rarely and reread them often. Done well, the narrative
becomes the answer to most roadmap arguments for the next year. Expect six to ten hours
across the week, mostly thinking.

## Before you start

You need your audience map from project 3.1.4, your interview synthesis from 3.2.5, and
your opportunity solution tree from 3.3.4. If you skipped any of them, do a quick version
now; a narrative without evidence is fiction. You also need the one-page press release from
lesson 4.1.1 and the loop sketch from 4.1.2.

Use prose, in a document, not slides. Aim for three to five pages for the narrative. The
map is a single diagram, drawn by hand or in Excalidraw, exported as an image.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Write the narrative.** Use these headings, in this order, and write in full sentences
   under each:
   - **Who and what.** The people you serve, the job they are trying to do, what gets in
     their way. Cite your interviews: how many, who, what they said. No claims without a
     source from Course 3.
   - **What has changed.** Why this is possible or necessary now, and not three years ago.
     Technology, regulation, behaviour, a competitor's mistake.
   - **The diagnosis.** In Rumelt's sense: the one or two obstacles or opportunities that
     matter most. One paragraph.
   - **The bet.** The wedge: which group, which problem, which first solution, using the
     four tests from lesson 4.1.2. State the guiding policy in one sentence.
   - **Why us.** The advantage you have or will build. If you have none yet, say what you
     would build first to get one. Honesty here is worth more than confidence.
   - **What we will not do.** At least three real things, with the reason. This is Porter's
     test, and a narrative that fails it is not finished.
   - **Success in twelve months.** Three to five measurable statements.
   - **What would make us wrong.** The three assumptions the bet most depends on, and the
     signal that would tell you each had failed.
2. **Draw the system map.** One diagram of your product's core loops: at least one
   reinforcing loop and one balancing loop, each box a state or action, each arrow labelled
   with what causes what. Mark the leverage point your bet pushes on, and mark where the
   wedge enters. Add a short caption, under a hundred words, explaining the diagram to
   someone who has not read the narrative.
3. **Run the four marks.** Reread the narrative with Rumelt's four marks of bad strategy
   from lesson 4.1.3. Add a short section at the end, "Where this is still weak", naming
   anything you found and could not fix. Reviewers will look here first.
4. **Submit.** Put the narrative, the map image and the press release from lesson 4.1.1
   in a folder called `4-1-4-narrative-and-system-map` in your work repository. Post the
   link in the project's Discussions thread using the template from lesson 0.3. Review two
   other narratives: for each, say which of the five Lafley and Martin questions it answers
   best and which it dodges.

#### Extra credit

- Write the narrative a second time in the five-choices format, one page. Compare the two
  and note what each version made you notice that the other hid.
- Give the narrative to someone who knows nothing about your product and ask them to
  explain the bet back to you in two sentences. Record how far off they were and what you
  changed as a result.

</div>
