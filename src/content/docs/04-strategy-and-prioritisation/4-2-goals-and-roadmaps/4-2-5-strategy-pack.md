## Introduction

This project turns the section into artifacts. You have a product idea from project 3.1.4,
an audience, interview evidence from 3.2.5, an opportunity tree from 3.3.4 and a narrative
from 4.1.4. Now you decide what to do about it: what success looks like next quarter,
which candidate solutions are worth the team's time, and in what order.

The strategy pack is the document a PM would bring to a planning session. It is also the
artifact hiring managers ask about most, because it shows whether you can turn research
into choices. Expect six to eight hours, more if you have not done the assignment drafts
from lessons 4.2.1 to 4.2.4.

## Before you start

Gather the drafts you made in this section's assignments: the objective and key results
from 4.2.1, the Now/Next/Later draft from 4.2.2, the scored list from 4.2.3 and the
planning process from 4.2.4. The project is mostly assembling and sharpening them, with one
new piece: a note on where your prioritisation framework misled you.

If your product idea has changed since 3.1.4, say so at the top and explain why. Changing
your mind on evidence is a good sign; the reviewers will want to see the evidence.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Goals sheet.** One page. Restate the strategy in two sentences (from 4.1.4), then one
   or two objectives for the next quarter with two to three key results each. For every key
   result give the baseline (or your best estimate and how you would get a real one), the
   instrument you would use to measure it, and a guardrail metric that must not get worse.
   No key result may be a feature.
2. **Candidate list.** Ten to fifteen candidate solutions drawn from your opportunity tree
   and interviews. For each: the problem it addresses, the key result it would move, and the
   evidence you have that it would.
3. **Prioritisation.** Score the candidates with one framework from 4.2.3 (RICE, ICE,
   weighted scoring or cost of delay). Show the inputs, not just the scores. Round the scores
   and mark ties as ties.
4. **Roadmap.** A Now/Next/Later roadmap built from the scored list. Three to five items per
   column. Each item carries the problem, the goal it serves, the candidate solution if any,
   who it is for, and a confidence. No dates beyond the current cycle. Something from your
   candidate list must be explicitly in a "Not doing" list with a reason.
5. **Framework note.** Three hundred words on what the framework got wrong. Where did the
   score disagree with your judgement, and which did you follow? Which input was pure
   guesswork? What would you need to learn to score with confidence? If the framework got
   nothing wrong, you have not looked hard enough.
6. **Submit.** Put the goals sheet, candidate list with scores, roadmap and note in a folder
   called `4-2-5-strategy-pack` in your work repository, with the planning process from
   lesson 4.2.4 as an appendix. Post the link in the project's Discussions thread using the
   template from lesson 0.3, and review two other strategy packs. In your reviews, pick one
   key result and ask whether the team could hit it by shipping nothing.

#### Extra credit

- Score the same candidate list with a second framework and add a column to the roadmap
  showing where the two orders disagree.
- Write the one-paragraph version of the roadmap you would send to a salesperson, and the
  one you would send to an executive, following lesson 4.2.2.

</div>
