## Introduction

You have the loop from lesson 1.2.1, the role boundaries from 1.2.2 and the business model
questions from 1.2.3. This project puts them on one real feature of one real product and
asks you to reconstruct, from the outside, how it travelled round the loop and why.

Teardowns are a core PM habit. Working PMs do them on competitors, on products they admire,
and on their own product after a launch. Doing one well means forming a hypothesis about
another team's reasoning from public evidence and being honest about what you cannot know.

## Before you start

Pick a product you use at least weekly and a specific feature of it that changed or appeared
in the last year or two. Not "the app" but "the new way it handles split payments" or "the
redesigned search results page". Smaller is better. If you cannot think of one, look at the
product's changelog, release notes, blog or app-store update history; most products have
one.

Gather what is public: release notes, blog posts about the change, press coverage, support
articles, reviews that mention it, the company's pricing page, and anything the company has
said about its strategy. Expect the research to take two to three hours and the writing two
more.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Frame the feature.** One paragraph: what the product is, what the feature does, when it
   appeared or changed, and how you can tell. Cite your sources with links.
2. **Trace it round the loop.** One section per stage, half a page each:
   - *Discovery.* What problem was this feature solving, and for whom? What evidence can you
     find that users had the problem (reviews, forum posts, support articles, competitor
     features)? What would the team have needed to learn before building it?
   - *Definition.* What do you think was in scope and what was deliberately left out? Where
     do you see trade-offs: a simpler version shipped, a setting omitted, a platform
     launched first? What success metric do you think the team chose?
   - *Delivery.* How did it ship: all at once, to some users first, behind a flag, by region?
     What does the rollout tell you about the team's confidence or constraints?
   - *Measurement.* What would the team be watching after launch? Has the feature changed
     since, and what does that suggest they learned?
3. **Connect it to the business.** Using the questions from lesson 1.2.3: who pays for this
   product, which unit-economics number does this feature most plausibly move (acquisition,
   retention, cost to serve, revenue per customer), and does the feature create value for
   users, capture revenue for the company, or both?
4. **Draw it.** One diagram of the loop for this feature, with your best guess at the key
   artifact or decision at each stage. Hand-drawn and photographed, or Excalidraw. It should
   fit on one screen.
5. **Say what you cannot know.** A short closing section listing the claims above that are
   guesses rather than evidence, and what you would need (data, an interview with the team)
   to confirm each. Honesty about uncertainty is a PM skill, and reviewers should look for
   it.
6. **Submit.** Put the write-up, the diagram and your source list in a folder called
   `1-2-4-lifecycle-teardown` in your work repository. Post the link in the project's
   Discussions thread using the template from lesson 0.3, and review two other teardowns.
   When reviewing, check whether the other person separated evidence from guesswork.

#### Extra credit

- Do the same teardown for the equivalent feature in a competing product and write half a
  page comparing the two teams' choices.
- If the product has a public changelog, chart how often it ships. Estimate the length of
  its loop and say what that implies about how it works.
