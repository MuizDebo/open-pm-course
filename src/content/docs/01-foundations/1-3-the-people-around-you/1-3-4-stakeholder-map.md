## Introduction

A stakeholder map is the first thing an experienced PM makes in a new role, usually before
touching the roadmap. It answers: who has a stake in this product, what do they want, what
are they afraid of, how much influence do they have, and how will I work with each? Done
well, it tells you where the support is, where the resistance will come from, and who you
need to talk to before any decision.

This project asks you to build one for a real or realistic product team, using everything in
this section: the three crafts from lesson 1.3.1, the five groups from 1.3.2, and the sources
of influence from 1.3.3.

## Before you start

Choose your product. Three options, in order of preference:

1. **A product team you are on or near.** Your own job, if you are a working PM or sit next
   to one. Anonymise names and anything confidential; roles are enough.
2. **A product you tore down in lesson 1.2.4.** You already know its business model and one
   of its features. Infer the team from what you can see: the size of the company, its
   public org structure, its job ads.
3. **A supplied case.** A fifty-person company selling scheduling software to small
   clinics, with a founder-CEO, a head of sales, six engineers, one designer, two support
   staff, a part-time compliance adviser, and you as the first PM. Make reasonable
   assumptions and state them.

Expect three to five hours.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **List the stakeholders.** Everyone with a stake in the product, by role rather than
   name. Aim for ten to fifteen. Include at minimum: the engineers (or their lead), the
   designer, whoever owns data, sales, marketing, support, legal or compliance, and at least
   two levels of leadership. Add the users themselves as a stakeholder group, and any
   external ones that matter (a key customer, a regulator, a platform you depend on).
2. **Build the map table.** One row per stakeholder, with these columns:
   - What they are measured on, in one line.
   - What they want from the product, and from you, in one line each.
   - What they fear: what a bad outcome looks like from their seat.
   - Influence over product decisions: high, medium or low, with a word on why.
   - Interest in day-to-day product work: high, medium or low.
   - How you will work with them: the cadence (weekly one-to-one, monthly update, as
     needed), the channel, and the one thing you will make sure they always hear from you
     first.
3. **Draw the grid.** Plot every stakeholder on a two-by-two: influence on one axis,
   interest on the other. Label the quadrants in your own words. The high-influence,
   low-interest corner is the one that catches PMs out; say who is in it and what you will
   do about it.
4. **Find the tensions.** Identify at least three pairs of stakeholders whose wants
   conflict (sales wanting a custom feature versus engineering wanting to pay down debt is
   the classic). For each, half a paragraph on how you would handle it, drawing on the four
   sources of influence from lesson 1.3.3. Be specific: what evidence you would bring, what
   you would write, who you would talk to first.
5. **Write the first month.** Half a page: if you joined this team as PM tomorrow, who
   would you talk to in week one, what would you ask them, and what would you write at the
   end of the month? Use the one-to-one questions from lesson 1.3.3's assignment.
6. **Submit.** Put the table, the grid and the write-up in a folder called
   `1-3-4-stakeholder-map` in your work repository. Post the link in the project's
   Discussions thread using the template from lesson 0.3, and review two other maps. When
   reviewing, look for stakeholders the other person missed, and whether their tensions are
   real conflicts or just different vocabularies for the same goal.

#### Extra credit

- Add a column for what each stakeholder would say about the PM role if asked privately,
  and whether it matches what they would say publicly.
- Revisit the map after Course 4 and mark which stakeholders your strategy document would
  need to be pre-wired with before it is presented.

</div>
