## Introduction

You have read four lessons about what the job is. This project checks that against what
companies actually hire for, in the market you would actually apply to, and ends with a
written position on where you fit. It is the first artifact in your portfolio and the one
you will reread most often.

A role audit is also something working PMs do every couple of years. The market moves, the
flavours shift, and the only way to know what the job is now is to go and read what people
are paying for.

## Before you start

You will need the three job ads from lesson 1.1.2 and the plotting you did in lesson 1.1.3.
This project extends them to eight ads and adds a written conclusion. Expect it to take four
to six hours including reading.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Collect eight product manager job ads.** Spread them deliberately:
   - At least two from companies in the country or region you would actually work in,
     including remote-friendly ones.
   - At least one consumer, one B2B, one platform or technical, and one AI or data role.
   - At least one early-stage startup and one large company.
   - Save each as a PDF or copy the text into your project folder, with the date and URL.
     Ads disappear.
2. **Build the audit table.** One row per ad, with these columns:
   - Company, role title, level (junior, mid, senior, lead) as best you can tell.
   - Product type, focus and company stage, using lesson 1.1.3's three axes.
   - Which historical layer it comes from, using lesson 1.1.2's decoder, with one quoted
     phrase as evidence.
   - The five most-emphasised responsibilities, in the ad's own words.
   - The five most-emphasised skills or requirements.
   - Anything that suggests the company does not really do product management (a long
     list of features to deliver, no mention of users or outcomes, "manage the backlog" as
     the headline).
3. **Find the patterns.** Below the table, write what you noticed in a few short
   paragraphs. Which responsibilities appeared in nearly every ad? Which skills? What varied
   most between flavours? Was there anything in the ads that none of the four lessons
   prepared you for?
4. **Write your position.** Three hundred words, in the first person, answering:
   - Which two or three of these roles would you most want, and why, in terms of the week
     you would live rather than the brand on the door?
   - What do you already have that those roles ask for? Be specific and honest.
   - What are the two biggest gaps between you and those roles today?
   - Include the memo from lesson 1.1.4's one-hour test as an appendix, and one sentence on
     whether that hour felt like work you want more of.
5. **Submit.** Put the ads, the table and the write-up in a folder called `1-1-5-role-audit`
   in your work repository. Post the link in the project's Discussions thread using the
   template from lesson 0.3, and review two other audits. Pay attention to whether the other
   person's "two biggest gaps" match what you would have guessed from their table.

#### Extra credit

- Add a ninth ad from a role you think you are not qualified for, two levels above where
  you would apply. Audit it the same way and note what changes between levels.
- Find the same company's PM ad from a few years ago on an archive site and compare. What
  moved?
