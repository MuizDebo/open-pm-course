## Introduction

You have a product idea with an audience map (3.1.4), interview findings (3.2.5), a
narrative (4.1.4) and a strategy pack with goals (4.2.5). This project turns the goals into
numbers: a metrics tree that says what success looks like and what drives it, and the
dashboard that would tell you every Monday whether you are getting there.

A metrics tree is the artifact most PMs are asked for in their first month and most have
never drawn. Doing it once for a product you know well makes the second one, at a job, a
morning's work rather than a week's anxiety.

## Before you start

Reread lesson 6.2 on metrics that matter and lesson 6.6 on dashboards. Have your strategy
pack open; the outcome goals in it are where the tree starts. If your product does not
exist yet, you are defining the metrics you would set up before launch, which is exactly
what a PM does.

Expect three to five hours. A whiteboard tool or paper and a phone camera are enough.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Choose the north star.** One metric that captures the value your product delivers to
   users, that moves when the product gets better, and that leads revenue rather than
   lagging it. Write it with a full definition: what counts, over what period, who is
   included. "Weekly active users" is not a definition. "Number of distinct users who
   completed at least one transfer in the trailing seven days" is.
2. **Build the tree.** Beneath the north star, list the four to six input metrics that
   drive it. For each, give a definition in the same style, say which team or decision can
   move it, and name the data source it would come from: an event, a table, a payment
   provider's export. Draw it as a tree with the north star at the top and arrows showing
   which inputs feed which.
3. **Add the guardrails.** Two or three metrics that must not get worse while you push the
   tree: a cost, a quality or trust measure, a support load. Mark them clearly as guardrails,
   not goals.
4. **Draw the dashboard.** One screen. The north star at the top with its trend and target.
   One chart per input metric in tree order, each titled with the question it answers.
   Guardrails along the bottom. For every chart, say what chart type and why. A sketch is
   fine; it does not need real data.
5. **Write the review script.** Half a page: how the Monday fifteen minutes would run with
   this dashboard, what "unusual" means for each metric, and who gets assigned what when
   something moves.
6. **Submit.** Put the tree, the dashboard sketch and the review script in a folder called
   `6-7-metrics-tree-and-dashboard` in your work repository. Post the link in the project's
   Discussions thread using the template from lesson 0.3, and review two others. For each,
   check whether their north star could be gamed by something that does not help users.

#### Extra credit

- Define each metric as a SQL query or a pivot table against the table layout you would
  expect to exist, using what you learned in lesson 6.5.
- Pick one input metric and write the experiment you would run to move it, in the form
  you will use in project 6.8.

</div>
