## Introduction

This project turns the three lessons in this section into the single document a working PM
produces most often. You will take the top item from your roadmap, write its requirements
document to the one-page discipline, break it into stories with acceptance criteria, and
attach wireframes. The result is the artifact interviewers most often ask to see and the one
engineers most often judge a PM by.

It is also the first project where your product idea from Course 3 meets the people who
would build it. Write it as if you were handing it to a real team on Monday.

## Before you start

You will need:

- Your product and audience from project 3.1.4, and the research from projects 3.2.5 and
  3.3.4.
- Your strategy pack from project 4.2.5. The feature for this project is the top item in
  the "Now" column of your roadmap.
- The half-page from lesson 5.1.1, the stories from lesson 5.1.2 and the wireframes from
  lesson 5.1.3, if you did those assignments. If not, this project includes them.

Expect six to ten hours. Most of it is thinking, not typing.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Write the first page.** On one page, no more, in this order: the problem and who has
   it (link your research, do not repeat it); the goal; one primary success metric with its
   current value, target and the date you would check; up to two secondary metrics; at least
   three non-goals, each with a one-line reason; the five most important requirements,
   numbered, written as outcomes; and the open questions, each with a name and a date. If it
   runs over a page, cut until it fits.
2. **Write the rest below the line.** The full requirements list, numbered, each one
   testable. Rollout: who gets it first, how you would turn it off, what you would watch in
   the first week. Risks: the three most likely ways this fails and what you would do.
   Dependencies on other teams or systems. Keep every item short.
3. **Break it into stories.** One epic per requirement on the first page. Five to twelve
   stories in total, each in the three-part form, each with three to six acceptance
   criteria. Use given/when/then for at least two stories where the logic is conditional.
   Mark which stories make up the smallest release you would be willing to ship.
4. **Add wireframes.** Two to four screens as wireframes, drawn in Excalidraw or on paper
   and photographed. Each captioned "sketch, not design". Place them beside the requirements
   they illustrate. One of them must show an error or empty state.
5. **Review it as an engineer.** Read the whole thing once pretending to be the engineer who
   will build it. Write down every question you would have to ask. Then either answer each
   in the document or move it to open questions. Record how many questions you found; put
   the number in your submission post.
6. **Submit.** Put the document, the stories and the wireframes in a folder called
   `5-1-4-the-prd` in your work repository, as Markdown or a shared document link. Post it
   in the project's thread with the template from lesson 0.3. When you review two other
   submissions, read their first page only and say whether you could build from it.

#### Extra credit

- Give the document to someone who writes code and ask them to estimate it in days, with
  the questions they had to ask first. Add their questions to a short appendix.
- Write a second version of the first page for a different audience: the executive who
  decides whether this gets funded. Note what changed and what did not.
