## Introduction

You specified a feature in project 5.1.4 and planned its build in project 5.2.6. This
project takes it the rest of the way: the plan for getting it into users' hands, the words
users will read when it arrives, and the honest look at how it might fail before it does.
Together they are a launch pack, which is what a PM hands to the rest of the company when
a feature is a few weeks from done.

A launch pack is also the artifact that most clearly separates PMs who ship features from
PMs who ship outcomes. Anyone can get code merged. The pack is the evidence that you
thought about who needs to know, what could go wrong, and how you will tell whether it
worked.

## Before you start

Have the PRD from 5.1.4 and the sprint plan from 5.2.6 open. Decide the launch tier first,
using lesson 5.4.1, and write one sentence justifying it. Everything else in the pack
scales with that decision. Expect four to six hours.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Launch plan.** One document, two to three pages, containing:
   - The tier and why.
   - The launch checklist: every row that must be true before go-live, with an owner role
     (you can invent the team) and a target date relative to launch day.
   - The rollout plan: stages, the percentage or segment at each, the signals that gate
     moving to the next stage, and what triggers a pause or rollback.
   - The enablement plan: what sales, support and success get, and when.
   - The success metric from your PRD, the dashboard or query that will show it, and the
     date you will review it.
2. **Release notes.** Two versions:
   - The user-facing note, under 150 words, following the rules in lesson 5.4.1: benefit
     first, who it applies to, limits, link to help.
   - The internal note for support and sales, under 300 words, with the five questions
     customers are most likely to ask and the honest answers.
3. **Pre-mortem.** Run one, with at least one other person if you can. Submit the ranked
   list of failure modes, the top five with their outcome (mitigation, signal, or accepted),
   and the changes you made to the launch plan as a result. If you ran it alone, say so
   and list what a second person would most likely have added.
4. **Retrospective artifact.** Attach the board photo or export and the action list from
   the retrospective you ran in lesson 5.4.2's assignment. Add two sentences on what you
   would do differently running it for a real product team.
5. **Submit.** Put all of it in a folder called `5-4-4-launch-pack` in your work
   repository. Post the link in the project's Discussions thread using the template from
   lesson 0.3, and review two other launch packs. When reviewing, look hardest at the
   pre-mortem: did they name the failure mode you would have named?

#### Extra credit

- Write the postmortem for your own launch as if it had failed in the way your pre-mortem
  ranked most likely, using the structure from lesson 5.4.3. Timeline, causes, actions.
  Then compare the actions with your mitigations: did the pre-mortem actually cover it?
- Record the two-minute enablement demo from lesson 5.4.1, using a clickable prototype or
  slides, and link it from the pack.

</div>
