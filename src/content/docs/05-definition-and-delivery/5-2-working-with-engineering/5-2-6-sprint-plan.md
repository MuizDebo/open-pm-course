## Introduction

You have a product requirements document from project 5.1.4. Engineers cannot build from a
PRD; they build from a backlog of stories small enough to finish and ordered so the valuable
ones come first. This project is the translation: PRD to epics, epics to vertically sliced
stories, stories into two sprints, with the risks and the definition of done written down.
Then you run sprint planning for real, with someone playing the team, because the plan is
only half the skill. The other half is the conversation.

Working PMs do this every two weeks. Doing it once with full attention is how you learn to do
it in twenty minutes later.

## Before you start

You will need:

- Your PRD from project 5.1.4, with its wireframes.
- The three-box sketch from lesson 5.2.1 and the risk list from lesson 5.2.3.
- The epic and six stories from lesson 5.2.5's assignment.
- A board. A physical wall with sticky notes, a free Trello or GitHub Projects board, a
  spreadsheet with columns. The tool does not matter; the columns do.
- A friend, colleague or fellow learner willing to spend forty-five minutes playing an
  engineer. Someone who has built software is better, but anyone who will ask "what exactly
  do you mean?" will do.

Expect five to seven hours in total.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Write the epics.** From the PRD, list every outcome the feature delivers as an epic, two
   to five of them. For each, write one sentence on what a user can do when it is done and
   which boxes from your 5.2.1 sketch it touches.
2. **Slice the stories.** Break each epic into vertically sliced stories using the patterns
   from lesson 5.2.5. Every story must pass "could someone use this?" and should be small
   enough to finish in a few days. Aim for twelve to twenty stories in total. Write each as:
   who, what they can do, and how you will know it works. No tasks; those belong to the team.
3. **Define done.** Write the team's definition of done for a story, in five lines or fewer,
   choosing from the five meanings in lesson 5.2.1. Say which environment, whether it is
   behind a flag, and whether it is measured.
4. **Order and plan two sprints.** Order the full list by value, with dependencies respected.
   Assume a team of three engineers and a two-week sprint, and assume a story takes one
   engineer two to four days. Draw the line for sprint one and sprint two. Write one sentence
   on what a user could do at the end of each sprint. If the answer for sprint one is
   "nothing yet", your slices are horizontal; cut again.
5. **Attach the risks.** From your 5.2.3 risk list, attach each risk to the story it
   threatens, and for the top three, write what you would do this week to retire it.
6. **Run sprint planning.** Forty-five minutes with your stand-in engineer. Walk the sprint one
   stories in order. For each, they ask questions until they could start building; you answer
   or write down what you do not know. Let them push back on size and order. Record every
   question you could not answer: that list is the gap between your PRD and a buildable
   backlog.
7. **Revise and write it up.** Fix the stories the planning exposed. Then write one page: what
   changed in planning, which questions you could not answer, what you would do differently
   in the PRD next time, and whether the sprint one line moved.

#### Extra credit

- Run a second planning session with a different person and compare the questions. The ones
  both people asked are holes in the spec; the ones only one asked are about the person.
- Estimate the stories in points with your stand-in using planning poker, then write down
  where you disagreed by more than double and why.
- Set up the board in GitHub Projects on your work repository, with WIP limits, so that it is
  public with the rest of your portfolio.

</div>

## Submitting your work

Post a link to your artifact in this project's thread in
[GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/MuizDebo/open-pm-course/discussions). Review at
least two other submissions while you are there. When reviewing, read the sprint one stories
and ask yourself whether you could start building from them; say which ones you could not,
and why.
