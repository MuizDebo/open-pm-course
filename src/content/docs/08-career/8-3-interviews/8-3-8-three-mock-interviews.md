## Introduction

Everything in section 8.3 is theory until someone asks you a question and watches you
answer it. This project puts you in the chair three times, with peers from the course on
the other side, and asks you to review your own performance honestly afterwards.

Mock interviews work for two reasons. The first is obvious: practice under mild pressure
beats reading about it. The second is less obvious and more valuable: sitting on the
interviewer's side teaches you what interviewers actually notice, which is rarely what
candidates worry about. You will interview as well as be interviewed.

## Before you start

You need the eight stories from lesson 8.3.5, your tap-the-button walkthrough from lesson
8.3.6, and at least a skim of the product sense, execution and strategy lessons earlier in
this section. You also need two or three partners.

Find them in the course's [Discussions](https://github.com/MuizDebo/open-pm-course/discussions).
Post in this project's thread saying which interview types you want to practise and your
time zone, and offer to interview in return. Pairing with someone at a similar stage is
fine. Pairing with someone further along is better. If nobody is available within a week,
a friend or colleague who has run interviews will do, with the question bank in the
assignment to work from.

Each interview is 45 minutes: 35 of questions and answers, 10 of feedback from the
interviewer while it is fresh. Record it, with your partner's explicit consent, so you can
review it. The recording is for you; you will not be asked to submit it.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Arrange three interviews**, each with a different interviewer if you can, covering:
   - A **product sense** interview: design or improve a product for a given user, as in
     lesson 8.3.2.
   - An **execution or strategy** interview: a metrics drop to diagnose, a prioritisation
     call, or a strategy question, as in lessons 8.3.3 and 8.3.4.
   - A **behavioural** interview: five or six "tell me about a time" questions drawn from
     different traits, as in lesson 8.3.5.
   Your interviewers can take questions from the
   [open PM interview question list](https://github.com/ferminrp/pm-interview-questions) or
   from the question bank at [Exponent](https://www.tryexponent.com/questions?type=behavioral).
2. **Interview your partners in return**, at least once each. Prepare properly: pick the
   questions in advance, take notes during, and give ten minutes of specific feedback at the
   end. Note what you found yourself noticing as an interviewer.
3. **Write a self-review of each interview**, one page or less, after watching the
   recording. Cover:
   - What went well, specifically. Not "I was confident", but "I clarified the user before
     proposing anything and the interviewer said that stood out."
   - What did not, specifically. Where you rambled, bluffed, skipped the result, or lost the
     thread. Timestamps help.
   - The gap between how it felt in the room and how it looked on the recording.
   - One thing you will change before the next interview. One, not five.
4. **Write a short closing note** across all three: which interview type is your weakest,
   what pattern showed up more than once, and what you noticed from the interviewer's chair
   that you had not understood as a candidate.
5. **Submit** the three self-reviews and the closing note in a folder called
   `8-3-8-three-mock-interviews` in your work repository. Do not submit the recordings
   unless you and your partner both want to. Post the link in this project's Discussions
   thread using the template from lesson 0.3, and review two other submissions. Read them
   for whether the self-criticism is specific or generic; say so kindly.

#### Extra credit

- Do a fourth interview, technical, using lesson 8.3.6's question types, with a partner who
  has an engineering background if you can find one.
- Repeat your weakest interview type two weeks later with a different partner, and add a
  paragraph to your closing note on what changed.
- If you are preparing for AI PM roles, make one of the three an AI PM interview using the
  questions from lesson 7.7.3.

</div>
