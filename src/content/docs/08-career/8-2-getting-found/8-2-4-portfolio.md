## Introduction

Everything you have made in this course has been accumulating in your work repository since
lesson 0.2. That repository is raw material. A portfolio is the edited version: the three to
six pieces that best show you can frame a problem, talk to users, make a call, write it down
and measure it, each with a short write-up that a hiring manager can read in two minutes.

This is the artifact that gets a PM hired without a PM title on their resume, and the one
working PMs use to show the thinking behind the outcomes on theirs.

## Before you start

You need your work repository, your one-page resume from lesson 8.2.1, and an honest view of
which projects you are proud of. Reread your "why you are here" note from lesson 0.1 first;
the portfolio should point at the kind of role it describes.

Choose the platform you can publish on today. GitHub Pages is free and lives next to your
repository; Notion's public pages are free and quick; a one-page site builder works too.
Nobody is grading the platform.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Pick three to six pieces.** Lead with judgement over effort. Strong choices from this
   course are the strategy pack (4.2.5), the five interviews and synthesis (3.2.5), the PRD
   (5.1.4), the experiment analysis (6.8), the AI opportunity audit or eval suite (7.2.4,
   7.4.6) and the working prototype (7.6.4). Include work from your job if you can sanitise
   it as lesson 0.3 describes.
2. **Write each piece up** in under three hundred words using four headings: the problem,
   what you did, the artifact (linked or embedded), and what you learned or would do
   differently. The last heading is the one hiring managers read most carefully. Be specific
   about mistakes.
3. **Write the front page.** Your name, the positioning line from your resume, two
   sentences on the kind of problems you want to work on, and the list of pieces. Link to
   your resume PDF and your work repository.
4. **Publish it.** Use [GitHub Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages)
   from your work repository, a public
   [Notion page](https://www.notion.com/help/public-pages-and-web-publishing), or a one-page
   builder such as [Carrd](https://carrd.co/). Check it on a phone.
5. **Make it findable.** Add the link to your resume, your LinkedIn About section and your
   GitHub profile. Put it in your Discussions introduction from lesson 0.3.
6. **Submit the link** and ask reviewers one question: which piece would they open first,
   and why?

#### Extra credit

- Add a short "how I work" page: how you run discovery, how you decide, how you handle
  disagreement, drawn from Courses 2 to 4. Hiring managers who read it will ask about it.
- Record a two-minute walkthrough of your strongest piece and embed it. Speaking about your
  work is interview practice, which section 8.3 covers.
- Publish one of the write-ups as a standalone post and share it in a community from lesson
  8.2.2. Note what happens.

</div>
