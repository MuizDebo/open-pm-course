## Introduction

Course 2 has been about the inside of the job: accountability, decisions under
uncertainty, communication, and the things that wear people down. This project closes it
with a single piece of writing. You write a letter to yourself at the moment you started
down this road, telling them what you now know.

If you are already a PM, the letter goes to the person on their first day. If you are not
yet, it goes to the person who opened lesson 0.1, with everything Courses 0 to 2 have
changed about how you see the job. Either way it is honest, specific and short.

Public submissions become a shared reading list in Discussions. Dozens of letters from
dozens of different starting points are a better lesson on what the job is really like than
anything a single author can write, including lesson 2.4.3.

## Why a letter

Three reasons this is a project and not a journal entry.

It forces you to pick. Everything you have read in Course 2 is true and most of it will not
matter to you personally. Writing to a specific person, you, at a specific moment, makes
you choose the three or four things that would actually have changed something.

It is a portfolio piece. Hiring managers ask "tell me about a time you were wrong" in every
PM interview. A written, reflective, specific account of what you learned the hard way is
the best preparation for that question there is, and Course 8 will ask you to reuse it.

It compounds. You will be asked to reread it in the capstone, and a good habit is to write a
new one every year. The distance between the letters is the clearest measure of growth you
will ever get.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Choose the moment.** Pick the exact point the letter is addressed to: your first day
   as a PM, the day you decided to become one, the day you took a particular job. Name it in
   the first line. A letter to "my younger self" is vague; a letter to "me, the Monday I
   joined the payments team" is not.
2. **Write 500 to 800 words.** No more. The constraint is the exercise. Cover, in whatever
   order works:
   - The two or three things you most wish you had known, stated as plainly as you can.
   - One mistake you made, or one you now see you were about to make, and what it cost.
   - One thing you were worried about that turned out not to matter.
   - One thing nobody warned you about. Lesson 1.1.4's list is a starting point, not a
     limit.
3. **Be specific.** "Talk to users earlier" is advice. "You will spend six weeks building
   the export feature because the sales lead said three customers asked for it, and none of
   them will use it, because you never asked what they needed the export for" is a letter.
   Change names and details as you need; keep the shape of what happened.
4. **Connect at least two points to the course.** A sentence each, noting which lesson in
   Courses 1 or 2 describes the pattern you fell into. This is for you, not for show: it is
   how you check that the abstract material has attached to something real.
5. **Submit.** Save the letter as `2-4-5-letter-to-your-past-self/letter.md` in your work
   repository. Post the link in this project's Discussions thread. Mark in your post whether
   you are happy for it to be included in the shared reading list. Then read at least three
   other letters and reply to one with a specific line that landed for you.

#### Extra credit

- Record yourself reading it, three to five minutes, and link the audio. Hearing your own
  account is a different experience from reading it, and it is good practice for the
  storytelling parts of PM interviews.
- Write the reply. One paragraph from your past self back to you, with the question they
  would ask. Answer it.

</div>
