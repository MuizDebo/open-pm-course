## Introduction

The strategy pack turns the discovery pack into a direction. It answers, in artifacts, the
questions a founder or a head of product would ask after reading your synthesis: where are
we going, how does this product work as a system, what are we trying to achieve this
quarter, what will we build first and in what order, and what are we deliberately not doing.

It draws on two Course 4 projects: 4.1.4 Narrative and system map and 4.2.5 Strategy pack.
If you made those for the product you are carrying forward, this pack is about making them
agree with the new discovery pack and with each other. If you are fresh or on Restock, it is
Course 4 applied in a week.

## Before you start

The test of a strategy pack is that a stranger can read the narrative and then predict what
is at the top of the backlog. If they cannot, the pieces do not agree. Before you submit,
hand the narrative to someone and ask them to guess your first feature.

**If reusing 4.1.4 and 4.2.5**, the goals sheet almost certainly needs rewriting: Course 6
will have taught you that two of your goals were outputs dressed as outcomes, and the new
discovery pack will have moved the wedge. Keep the system map's shape if it still holds, but
redraw the loops with what the interviews told you about where value actually accrues.

**For Restock**, the system has two sides and a trust problem in the middle. Your system
map should show the loop that gets a shop owner to order a second time and the loop that
gets a wholesaler to fulfil reliably, and where those loops touch. Your narrative has to
explain why WhatsApp-first is the right wedge and what you give up by choosing it. Your
roadmap has six months of runway to respect.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Narrative.** One to two pages in the form from lesson 4.1.1 Vision, strategy,
   narrative: the world as it is, the change you see, what the product is, why now, why
   you, and what success looks like in eighteen months. Plain prose. No bullet points. If
   you prefer the press-release form, use it, but include the hard paragraph about what the
   product does not do.
2. **System map.** The product's core loops drawn as a diagram, with the wedge marked and
   the single point where you believe value compounds. Half a page of notes on what breaks
   the loop. Redraw from 4.1.4 rather than reusing it.
3. **Goals sheet.** Two or three outcome goals for the first two quarters, each with a
   metric, a current or baseline value if you have one, and a target. Say which goal the
   first feature serves. Outcomes, not outputs; lesson 4.2.1 Goals and OKRs is the test.
4. **Prioritised backlog.** Eight to fifteen candidate features scored with one framework
   from lesson 4.2.3 Prioritisation frameworks, with the scores shown. Below the table, a
   paragraph on what the framework got wrong and what you overrode.
5. **Now, Next, Later roadmap.** Themes, not dates, with the Now column tied to the goals
   sheet. One line under it on how you would present this to a sales lead and to an
   investor, and what you would say differently to each.
6. **Decision record.** One page: the strategic bet you are making, the alternative wedge
   you considered, and what evidence would make you switch.
7. **Pack summary.** A `README.md` in `9-capstone/strategy`: two sentences per artifact and
   the link to each.

#### Extra credit

- Write the one-page memo from project 2.3.5 The hard memo for the feature you cut that
  someone in your interviews most wanted.
- Run the goals sheet through a pre-mortem: assume it is six months on and every goal was
  missed. Write the three most likely reasons.

</div>

## Submitting your work

Post a link to your artifact in this project's thread in
[GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/MuizDebo/open-pm-course/discussions). Review at
least two other submissions while you are there. For capstone packs, try the stranger test
on someone else's narrative: read it, guess their first feature, then check their backlog,
and tell them whether you got it right.
