## Introduction

Six packs of work sit in your repository. Nobody who matters will read all of them. What
they will do is give you ten minutes, and in those ten minutes they will decide whether you
think like a product manager. The pitch is the practice run for that moment: a recorded
walkthrough of the entire case, in order, with the reasoning visible.

This is not a sales pitch and it is not a demo. It is the talk a PM gives at a product
review when the leadership team asks "walk us through it". The best ones are calm, honest
about what is uncertain, and make the listener feel that every decision had a reason.

## Before you start

Watch two or three product reviews or launch talks on video before you record, with the
sound on and a notebook open. Note how long the speaker spends on the problem before they
mention a solution. It is usually longer than you expect. Then open all six packs and write
the one sentence each pack contributes to the story. If a pack does not contribute a
sentence, you will not mention it, and that is fine.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Structure the talk.** Ten minutes, in this order, with roughly these timings: the
   problem and who has it (two minutes, from your discovery pack, with one real quote from an
   interview); the audience and wedge (one minute); the strategy and what you are saying no
   to (one minute); the first feature and why that one (two minutes, from your delivery
   pack); how you will know it worked (one minute, your north star and the first experiment);
   where AI belongs and where it does not (one minute, from your AI pack, including the
   audit result even if it was "not yet"); the risks that keep you up at night and what comes
   next (two minutes). Write the script or detailed notes before you record.
2. **Record it.** A phone propped against a mug, a laptop webcam or a screen recorder with
   your voice over slides are all acceptable. Slides are optional; if you use them, no more
   than eight, and your face or voice must carry the argument, not the slides. Record it in
   one take after at least two practice runs. Do not edit out the stumbles. Ten minutes
   means between nine and eleven.
3. **Watch it back once** with the peer review rubric below and write five lines on what you
   would change. Do not re-record unless the audio was unusable. The point is the practice,
   not the production.
4. **Submit the link** to the recording (unlisted YouTube, Loom, Google Drive with link
   sharing, or similar) along with the script or notes and your five lines of self-review.

#### Extra credit

- Present it live to someone who has hired product managers. Write up every question they
  asked and how you answered, then note which questions your packs had already answered
  and which caught you out.
- Record a three-minute version. Cutting the same case to a third of the length teaches you
  which parts were load-bearing.

</div>

## The peer review rubric

When you review someone else's pitch, and when you review your own, score each line from
one to three and leave one sentence of evidence for each:

- **Problem first.** Did I understand who has the problem and why it matters before any
  solution appeared?
- **Reasoning visible.** For the two or three biggest decisions, could I tell why that
  option and not the others?
- **Honest about uncertainty.** Did the speaker name what they do not know and what would
  change their mind?
- **Measurement is real.** Could I say, after the talk, what number would mean success?
- **AI judgement.** Was the AI decision argued from the users and the constraints, not from
  fashion?
- **Time and clarity.** Did it land between nine and eleven minutes, and could I follow it
  without the packs?

A score of two across the board is a good pitch. Threes everywhere is rare and usually means
the reviewer was being kind.

Post a link to your artifact in this project's thread in
