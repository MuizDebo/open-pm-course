## Introduction

The discovery pack is the evidence base for everything that follows. The strategy pack
will claim a wedge; this pack is where the wedge has to be visible in what real people
said. The delivery pack will specify a first feature; this pack is where the problem that
feature solves has to appear, in someone's words, more than once.

It draws on three Course 3 projects: 3.1.4 Audience map, 3.2.5 Five interviews and 3.3.4
Opportunity solution tree. If you did those on the product you are now carrying into the
capstone, this pack is about integrating and tightening them. If you are starting fresh or
working the Restock case, this pack is the full discovery cycle from Course 3, done in a
week.

## Before you start

**If you are reusing earlier work**, "integrated and tightened" means three things. First,
the three artifacts have to agree: every opportunity in the tree should trace to at least
one interview, and every segment on the audience map that you chose as the wedge should be
the segment you actually interviewed. Where they do not agree, fix the artifact, not the
story. Second, reread the review comments each one received and act on them. Third, apply
what you learned later: Course 4 will have changed how you think about the wedge, Course 6
about what you would measure, Course 7 about whether any opportunity is an AI opportunity.
Expect to redraw the tree.

**If you are working the Restock case**, your audience is not "shop owners". It is a
specific kind of shop owner, in specific areas, buying specific categories from specific
wholesalers, and the wholesalers are an audience too. Your interviews should include both
sides. Five is the minimum; for a two-sided product, eight split across both sides is
better. You will not find these people online. Walk.

**If you are starting fresh with your own idea**, follow Course 3 in order and give the
interviews most of the week.

## Assignment

<div class="assignment">

1. **Audience map.** Segments, early adopters, a rough size for each, and the wedge you are
   choosing, with the reason in two sentences. If reusing 3.1.4, mark what changed and why.
   For Restock, map both sides of the market and say which side you would win first and
   why.
2. **Interviews.** At least five, on the problem your product statement names. Submit the
   script, anonymised notes for each, and a one-page synthesis with three to five insight
   statements, each backed by at least two interviews. If reusing 3.2.5, add at least two new
   interviews that test the opportunity you plan to pursue, and show how the synthesis
   changed. For Restock, at least two interviews must be with wholesalers.
3. **Opportunity solution tree.** Outcome at the top, opportunities beneath drawn from the
   synthesis, two or three solution ideas under the opportunity you are pursuing, and the
   assumption tests you would run first. Every opportunity cites an interview. If reusing
   3.3.4, redraw it rather than editing it; what you keep tells you what held up.
4. **Decision record.** One page: which opportunity you are taking into the strategy pack,
   which strong contender you rejected, and what would have to be true for you to reverse
   the choice. This is the hard decision reviewers will look for.
5. **Pack summary.** A `README.md` in the `9-capstone/discovery` folder: two sentences per
   artifact and the link to each. Written for someone with two minutes.

#### Extra credit

- Run one assumption test from the tree before moving on: a landing page, a concierge
  version, a fake door, five more conversations. Report what happened in a paragraph.
- Size the wedge bottom-up with the method from lesson 3.3.1 Market sizing and compare it
  with the top-down number from your audience map. Say which you trust and why.

</div>

## Submitting your work

Post a link to your artifact in this project's thread in
[GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/MuizDebo/open-pm-course/discussions). Review at
least two other submissions while you are there. For capstone packs, say in your review
whether the tree traces back to the interviews, and name the decision you found most
interesting.
